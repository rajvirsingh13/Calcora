How Is BMI Calculated?

BMI is calculated by dividing a person's weight in kilograms by the square of their height in meters.

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ [Height (m) × Height (m)]

For example, if a person weighs 65 kg and is 1.70 m tall, the BMI is calculated as 65 ÷ (1.70 × 1.70), which is approximately 22.5.

Understanding BMI

BMI is commonly used as a screening measure for adults. The standard categories shown by this calculator are general guidelines and should not be treated as a complete assessment of a person's health.

Factors such as muscle mass, body composition, age, growth and individual health can affect how useful BMI is for a particular person.

For children and teenagers, BMI is interpreted differently because growth and development are still occurring. A healthcare professional can provide age- and growth-appropriate guidance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does BMI stand for?

BMI stands for Body Mass Index. It is a number calculated from height and weight and is commonly used as a general screening measure.

What is the BMI formula?

The standard BMI formula is weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared.

Is BMI an exact measure of health?

No. BMI is a screening tool and does not directly measure body fat, muscle mass or overall health.

Can BMI be used for children and teenagers?

BMI is interpreted differently for children and teenagers. Standard adult BMI categories should not be used to assess growing children or teens.
